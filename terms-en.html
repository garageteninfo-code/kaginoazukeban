Terms of use and privacy

鍵の預け番 Kagi-no-azukeban — Paper cards that split an emergency note among several trusted people (free prototype).

This English text was translated with AI and has not been checked by a native speaker. If this English version and the Japanese version differ, the Japanese version (利用条件) is the original. The tool's screens are in Japanese only.

1. What this tool does

It encrypts the note you write and splits the key into several cards (Shamir's secret sharing and the browser's built-in AES-GCM encryption). The note can be read only when the chosen number of cards is brought together. Encrypting, splitting and opening happen only inside your web browser.

2. We do not collect or send your data

  • Your note, company or shop name, the names you give the card holders, the card codes, the envelope text and the log are not sent anywhere, including to the provider. The page contains no code that sends data over the internet, and its security settings (Content Security Policy) block network access.
  • The page does not store your note or cards in your browser (it does not write to localStorage or similar). They are gone when you close the page. Only when you press a "print" or "save" button does your browser send them to your printer or save a file to your computer.
  • The provider holds no copy of your note, cards or records. If cards or the envelope are lost, the provider cannot open the note for you.
  • This page is served from GitHub Pages (a free hosting service of GitHub, Inc.). GitHub may keep connection records (such as IP addresses) of people who open the page, for security purposes. Please see that company's privacy statement. The provider does not view or use those records.

3. No guarantee

  • This tool has not been reviewed by a cryptography expert. It uses the browser's standard random numbers and encryption (Web Crypto).
  • Anyone who collects the chosen number of cards can read the note. The tool cannot protect the paper cards themselves. Give each card to a different person or place. If fewer than the chosen number of cards remain, nobody can read the note.
  • The printed layout has not been checked on a real printer and paper. After making the cards, always try opening them once.
  • The tamper-evident log cannot detect deletion of its last line, or a complete rewrite of everything after the "log mark" printed on the cards.
